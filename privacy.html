Privacy and terms

Last updated: November 20, 2026

This page explains what the masUNO app collects, who can see it, and the rules for using the app. For the waitlist on this website, see the waitlist privacy notice.

Who we are

masUNO helps university students find plans, like pickup basketball, tennis, soccer, volleyball and padel, that are one or two people short. You can host a plan or join one on a map. We're launching at Tec de Monterrey.

We're a four-person student team, not a company.

You can reach us at official.masuno.app@gmail.com.

What we collect

When you create an account

You create an account with your school email address (at Tec, your @tec.mx address) and a password. We store your email and an account ID. Your password is stored only as a secure hash by our sign-in provider; we can't see it.

masUNO is invite-only for now. If you use a member's invite code, we record whose code it was. If you join the waitlist, we keep your place in line until you're let in.

Your profile

  • Your first name, last name, class year and the sports you play.
  • The dates you agreed to the community guidelines and the chat rules.
  • Your language setting.

Your plans

  • Plans you host: title, activity, date and time, duration, number of people, place name, the place's address and map coordinates, any meet-up spot or notes you add, and whether auto-repost is on.
  • Plans you join, and when you joined or left.
  • Messages you send in a plan's group chat, the people you @mention, and when you last opened each chat.

Safety

  • Reports you make: a reason, an optional note and, if you report a chat message, a copy of that message.
  • Blocks you make.

Saved plans, notifications and invites

  • Saved plans and alerts. The plans you save and the spot alerts you turn on, stored with your account.
  • Notifications. If you allow notifications: your device's notification token, an ID for this install of the app, the app version and language, your notification settings, and which notifications you open.
  • Invites. Your invite codes and who used them.

What stays on your phone

  • Your location. The app uses it on your phone to center the map and show how far plans are. It's sent to us only if you host a plan and choose “Use my current location” as the place: that spot is then saved with the plan like any other place.
  • Your calendar. Add to calendar puts a plan in your calendar only when you confirm it. We can't read your calendar.

What we don't collect

  • Ads and tracking. There are no ads, no third-party analytics tools and no tracking. We do count which notifications get opened, to learn which ones are useful.

We don't sell your data or share it for marketing.

Crash reports reach us only through Apple, and only from people who've opted in on their iPhone.

Why we use it

  • Your school email lets you sign in and shows you're a student at your campus. We use it only for that.
  • Your name and class year show other students who's hosting or going.
  • Your plan details put plans on the map and let people join them.
  • Your messages let people in a plan work out the details together. We keep track of when you last opened a chat so we can show how many messages are new.
  • Reports and blocks help us keep masUNO safe and enforce the rules below.
  • Your language setting picks the language of the app and its notifications. The sports you play decide which new plans we tell you about.
  • Your notification token and settings let us send the notifications you've turned on, and nothing else.

Who sees what

  • Signed-in students at your campus can see your first name, last initial (like “Emilio G.”) and class year, the plans you host with the details listed above, and the plans you're going to.
  • People in the same plan can also read the messages you send in its group chat and see when you joined or left.
  • No other user sees your email, your full last name or the sports you play.
  • People you block don't see your name, the plans you host or your messages, and you don't see theirs.
  • Only the masUNO team sees reports.

Our team runs the database, so we can see what's stored there, including group chat messages.

Where your data is stored and who helps us run masUNO

  • Supabase runs our database, sign-in and group chat on servers in the United States. Access rules limit what each signed-in student can read.
  • Apple distributes the app, provides the map and place search, and delivers notifications.
  • Discord: when a report is made, an alert goes to our team's private channel. It has the reason, what kind of thing was reported and a report number. It doesn't include names or message text.
  • Google (Gmail and Google Sheets) handles the email you send us and our waitlist: if you joined the waitlist, your email and place in line are kept in a Google Sheet only our team can open.

If you choose Google Maps for directions, the plan's place opens in that app.

Keeping and deleting your data

We keep your account data until you delete your account. Chat messages are deleted 30 days after their plan ends. We keep reports for as long as masUNO is running, so we can spot repeat problems.

To delete your account, go to Settings › Delete account. It takes effect right away and:

  • Deletes your profile and the sign-in record that holds your name and school email.
  • Removes the plans you host, along with their group chats.
  • Removes you from plans you joined, and deletes the messages you sent.
  • Deletes your blocks.
  • Deletes your saved plans, notification settings and notification tokens.
  • Deletes your place on the waitlist.

Reports are kept for moderation, but they're no longer linked to your account. That covers reports you made and reports about you, including the copy of a reported message.

If you can't sign in, email us from your school address and we'll delete your account for you.

Community rules and safety

masUNO is for university students who are 17 or older. To use it, you need a school account from a campus where masUNO is open.

Everyone agrees to these rules before using the app:

  • Be respectful. No harassment, hate or slurs.
  • Keep it about the plan: timing, where to meet, who's bringing what.
  • Don't share anyone's personal info or photos without asking.
  • Meet in public places and look out for each other.

Spam, fake plans and inappropriate content aren't allowed either. Hosts can't post a plan more than 7 days ahead. A text filter checks names, plan titles, notes, meet-up spots and chat messages.

You keep ownership of what you post on masUNO: plan names, notes, meet-up spots, messages and your profile name. By posting it, you give masUNO permission to store it and show it to other students in the app, for as long as it's on masUNO. That permission ends when you delete the content or your account, except for copies kept with reports for moderation.

You can report or block anyone from the Who's going list, report a plan, and report a message in a group chat. We review reports within 24 hours. We may remove content, cancel plans, clear offensive names or suspend accounts.

You take part at your own risk and are responsible for your own safety at plans. In an emergency, call 911 (it works in Mexico and the US) or your campus security.

Your choices

  • Location: say no when the app asks, or change it anytime in your iPhone's Settings. Without it, the map centers on your campus.
  • Notifications: turn each kind on or off in Settings › Notifications, or all of them in your iPhone's Settings.
  • Your profile: change your name, class year and sports in the app.
  • Blocking: block anyone from the Who's going list, and undo it under Settings › Blocked people.
  • Deletion: go to Settings › Delete account.

Your rights

You can ask to access the data we have about you, correct it, have it deleted, or object to how we use it. These are known in Mexico as your ARCO rights. You can also withdraw your consent at any time.

To do any of this, or to ask anything about your data, email official.masuno.app@gmail.com from your school address. We'll answer within the time limits set by law.

If you think we haven't respected your rights, you can contact the Mexican authority in charge of personal data protection.
